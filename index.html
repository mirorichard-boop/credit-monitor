Methodology & Framework

Core Credit Stress Score

A weighted average of each series' long-run percentile: a value at the 90th percentile means spreads are wider than 90% of history. Weights (0–10) are shown as bars. CCC & Below is excluded by default; see the CCC Distress Gauge below. The figure under the score shows the same weights ranked only against each series' own 3-year window, the method this dashboard used before.

Long-History Anchoring

FRED serves only ~3 years of the ICE BofA OAS indices. The Moody's Aaa and Baa spreads to the 10-year Treasury (AAA10Y, BAA10Y) go back to the 1980s. Each ICE series is ranked within its own window; the Moody's series is read at the same rank over the same dates; that Moody's value is then ranked against its full history. AAA/AA/A map to Moody's Aaa, everything else to Moody's Baa. This assumes each ICE series sits in its long-run range roughly where Moody's does over the same 3 years. That is a reasonable but untested assumption for high yield, since Moody's covers investment grade only. Moody's Aaa is also partly a curve measure (its bonds are longer than 10 years), so the AAA/AA/A readings are the least reliable and carry the smallest weights.

CCC Distress Gauge

CCC spreads can blow out on a handful of distressed names or restructurings without broader credit following, as in the 2015–16 energy episode. The gauge tracks the CCC − single-B gap. When that gap ranks at or above its 80th percentile, CCC is ISOLATED unless at least one higher-quality check confirms: single-B or HY Master long-run stress ≥ 60, single-B widening ≥ 75 bps in a month, or BB widening ≥ 50 bps in a month. Once CONFIRMED, CCC enters the composite at weight 6.

Zone Thresholds

Clear (0–25): Normal or compressed spreads, typical of bull markets. Watch (25–50): Mild widening, mid-cycle. Caution (50–75): Significant widening, often pre-crisis. Danger (75+): Historically extreme levels, associated with recessions and credit events.

Weights

BBB, Moody's Baa, BB and single-B carry the most weight (7/10): they are the largest pools of credit and where fallen-angel and default risk first shows up broadly. 10Y–3M (8/10) is the NY Fed's preferred recession predictor. AAA/AA/A carry little weight because they rarely signal anything on their own.

Curve Scoring

Treasury curve spreads are scored inversely — more negative (inverted) = higher stress. An inversion in 10Y–3M has preceded every U.S. recession since the 1960s. The Pctile column shows where the curve ranks in its own history; its color, the section badge, and the composite use the inverted (stress) reading.

Data Source

FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis): ICE BofA OAS indices for IG and HY, Moody's seasoned Aaa and Baa yields less the 10-year Treasury, Treasury constant-maturity spreads for the curve, and the Freddie Mac 30-year mortgage rate. A GitHub Action pulls FRED every weekday at 6pm Pacific, merges new observations into the archive, and recomputes the scores.
