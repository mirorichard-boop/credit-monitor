Methodology & Framework

Composite Stress Score

The score is a sensitivity-weighted average of percentile ranks for each series vs. its full archived history. Each series' current value is ranked against every observation in the archive; a value at the 90th percentile means spreads are wider than 90% of archived observations. These percentiles are then weighted by each series' sensitivity score (1–10) and averaged to produce the composite. The date range under each instrument name is the window it is ranked against.

Zone Thresholds

Clear (0–25): Normal or compressed spreads, typical of bull markets. Watch (25–50): Mild widening, mid-cycle. Caution (50–75): Significant widening, often pre-crisis. Danger (75+): Historically extreme levels, associated with recessions and credit events.

Sensitivity Weights

CCC-rated HY (10/10) is the most sensitive — it moves first and furthest in stress. BBB IG (7/10) carries the most fallen-angel risk. 10Y–3M curve (8/10) is the NY Fed's preferred recession predictor. The mortgage spread (5/10) proxies consumer credit conditions. EM corporate (6/10) captures global contagion. These weights ensure that a calm IG surface can't mask HY distress.

Curve Scoring

Treasury curve spreads are scored inversely — more negative (inverted) = higher stress. An inversion in 10Y–3M has preceded every U.S. recession since the 1960s. The Pctile column shows where the curve ranks in its own history; its color, the section badge, and the composite use the inverted (stress) reading.

Short History Windows

FRED now serves only the trailing ~3 years of the ICE BofA OAS indices. A short window makes percentiles relative to recent conditions only: after three calm years, a modest widening can rank near the 100th percentile. This site's archive keeps every observation it has collected, so the ICE windows lengthen by one day each business day. Windows shorter than 10 years are shown in orange.

Data Source

FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis): ICE BofA OAS indices for IG and HY, Treasury constant-maturity spreads for the curve, and the Freddie Mac 30-year mortgage rate. A GitHub Action pulls FRED every weekday at 6pm Pacific, merges new observations into the archive, and recomputes the scores.
