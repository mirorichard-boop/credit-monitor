Methodology, sources, and where this differs from CrossBorder

What this is

A public-data proxy for Michael Howell's global liquidity framework (CrossBorder Capital, Capital Wars). CrossBorder's Global Liquidity Index covers ~90 financial systems, including shadow banks and cross-border flows, and is a paid product. This page rebuilds the parts visible in free data and runs them through his cycle logic.

Liquidity Cycle Index (quantities)

Howell's model uses quantities only; he treats bond volatility and spreads as outputs. Inputs, each a rolling 5-year z-score:

  • Fed net liquidity (balance sheet − Treasury General Account − reverse repo), 26-week % change, weight 2
  • G3 central-bank liquidity (Fed net + ECB + BoJ in USD), 52-week % change, weight 2
  • Bank reserves, 26-week % change, weight 1
  • "Treasury QE": bills as a share of marketable debt, 52-week change, weight 1. Shorter issuance adds liquidity, because liquidity ≈ size ÷ duration.
  • Broad dollar, 26-week % change, inverted, weight 1 (stands in for cross-border flows)

The weighted z-score is mapped to 0–100 through the normal CDF. 50 is trend. The headline is a 13-week average.

Phases

Level above or below 50, crossed with the direction of the 26-week change: Rebound (below, rising), Calm (above, rising), Speculation (above, falling), Turbulence (below, falling). A new phase is adopted only after the raw signal has held for 6 straight weeks. Without that rule the phase flips every few weeks; with it, phases average ~33 weeks.

Plumbing stress (prices)

MOVE (weight 1.5), SOFR − IORB, 13-week change in Baa − 10Y, Chicago Fed NFCI, and VIX (weight 0.5). This measures how hard the system is straining to roll its debt, kept separate from the liquidity cycle.

Debt ↔ liquidity nexus

From Howell (BTC Sessions, Oct 2026). If debt can't be turned into liquidity, MOVE spikes and SOFR spreads widen. If liquidity can't be turned into debt, term premia compress and credit spreads widen. Either one firing is a refinancing squeeze.

Reserve adequacy

Goldman's measure: (bank reserves + overnight reverse repo) ÷ total commercial bank assets (H.8). Zones: 13%+ abundant (NY Fed found the funds rate stops responding to reserves above ~13%), 11–13% ample, 10–11% the steep part of the demand curve, under 10% scarce. Sept 2019 broke near 7–8%. The price check is repo against the Fed's floor: TGCR (NY Fed) and SOFR minus IORB, plus Standing Repo Facility usage. Past episodes are found from the data: days SOFR printed 10 bp or more above the floor, grouped when within 45 days.

Known gaps

  • No PBoC. Howell says US and Chinese cycles have run in opposite phase since ~2020, so a G3 proxy can read differently from his global number.
  • No private-sector or shadow-bank balance sheets.
  • The debt/liquidity ratio here is US federal debt ÷ G3 central-bank liquidity. Its level is not comparable to Howell's global ~2× equilibrium, so only its percentile is meaningful.
  • The 0–100 scaling uses the full-sample spread of the composite. That changes scale, not ranking, but the backtest is not strictly out of sample.
